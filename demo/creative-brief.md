# The Unlisted Project: MinuteProof (Board Minutes Due Diligence) — creative brief

Status: settled by the user through the team lead on 2026-11-04; remaining choices are labelled assumptions. Planned items are expectations to be checked by a capture. "Observed" means read on 2026-11-04 from the live API (`http://127.0.0.1:4173/api/due-diligence/{summary,recurring,red-flags,spend}`), which matches the complete Reducto extracts.

- **Audience and takeaway:** judges and attendees of a one-hour Reducto hackathon, **including viewers new to all of it**. Takeaway, in the user's words: "Hundreds of pages of board minutes became a cited red-flag report in minutes." The pain: a home buyer, funder or auditor gets hundreds of pages of HOA or board minutes before a deadline and can't read them all, so special assessments, deferred repairs and disputes stay buried.
- **Product name (the user's decision):** "create a product name and add that to the intro and outro, like 'this is XXXX'". The coordinator chose **MinuteProof**, tagline "Red flags from board minutes, with proof." The intro card's headline is the name and its narration says "This is MinuteProof" right after the pain beat; the outro headline starts with the name and its narration opens "That's MinuteProof." MinuteProof is the demo's name for this page and its pipeline, built on Reducto and DuckDB; nothing implies a shipped product beyond the demo.
- **Accessible to a novice (the user's decision):** "make the script more accessible and explanatory to a novice." Every term is defined in plain words the first time (board minutes, due diligence, red flag, citation, Reducto, DuckDB, piles, deferred); no unexplained jargon ("extract", "schema", "endpoint", "pipeline" are avoided in narration); short sentences, one idea each; the narration says where to look. The how is told in four plain steps: Reducto reads every page; it pulls out each decision, repair, dollar amount and dispute and notes its page; DuckDB groups them over time to find what keeps coming back; every claim links to its page so you can check it.
- **Big picture (the user's decision):** "Since this is a hackathon demo, also focus on the big-picture value; it can even be forward-looking." The frame widens beyond Vaucluse House: the same approach could work on any pile of minutes (HOA packets before a home closing, city council and school board minutes, nonprofit board books, lender due diligence), toward "every buyer gets a cited due-diligence report in minutes". Rules: vision lines are worded as vision ("imagine…", "next…", "could…"), spoken only over the outro card, never as shipped features; anything stated as done must be true on the page. The Vaucluse walkthrough stays in the middle as the proof.
- **Core outcome:** the "Board Minutes Due Diligence" page (`/due-diligence.html`) turns the Vaucluse House trust minute books (1972–73, 298 pages; 1974–75, 420 pages) into recurring issues and red flags, each with its source paragraph and a link to the exact PDF page. Observed: 718 pages, 38 meetings, 968 decisions, 128 red flags, 134 deferred or unresolved, $59,252 approved spend, ≈ 24 hours of reading saved, extracted in ≈ 6 minutes across 12 parallel Reducto jobs, 1972-02-20 to 1975-06-15.
- **Length (target / ceiling) and where it will be seen:** target 3 minutes, 180 s (the user's decision: "target 3 minutes for the demo video"); ceiling 200 s (assumption); both cards included. Current estimate: ≈ 193 s at 2.4 words/s, ≈ 180 s at 2.6 (391 spoken words). Shown to judges at the hackathon.
- **Format:** a focused outcome story (user's choice), framed by an intro and an outro card: problem → the Vaucluse proof → the vision. The extra time goes to what is already built and verified on the page, explained slowly.
- **Tone and viewpoint:** conversational, second person ("you get…") (assumption), plain and explanatory.
- **In scope:** intro card; hero result; the six stat tiles and reading-time pill; the "Every page, read" page map; "What keeps coming back" (the wharf in depth: counts, timeline dots, the latest "deferred" pile stump); one cited red flag and its original PDF page; two more recurring issues briefly (white ants, the kitchen restoration); the category filter buttons; the spend chart; the how-it-works strip with the extraction time; outro card.
- **Out of scope:** the Reducto API key, terminals, the extraction run itself, unrelated Unlisted Project listings, and any vision presented as a working feature. The listing opening is optional and cut at this length (it would pass the ceiling); the theme switch is not used.
- **Voice and delivery (the user's decision):** Heart (`af_heart`), Kokoro's local American female stock voice, at Kokoro speed 1.0. Room tone, reverb and microphone character are all explicitly **on** and subtle; the explicit on wins over the runtime's voice-aware `auto`. The audio stage confirms that the profile is installed and inspects `narrate --show-settings` before narrating; the narration still needs a listening review. Timing is budgeted at a conservative 2.4 words/s (af_heart at speed 1.0 runs about 2.4–2.6), with real holds so a novice can read what is named.
- **Captions and chapter titles:** both off (the user's decision, unless they say otherwise).
- **Cards and images (the user's decisions):** "Once ready, include a start and end slide with intro and summary." The intro card "should explain what the system does and what the demo is going to show": in order, the pain, what the system does, and a preview. The outro card summarizes what you saw (718 pages → cited red flags), the stack (Reducto + DuckDB) and the vision (a cited due-diligence report for every buyer, in minutes), and **ends with "Thank you for watching"**, held long enough to be read after the narration ends. Cards are not chapters and hold no result claims: their text is factual or clearly visionary. Exact text and holds are in `script.md`.
- **Highlights:** restrained and timed to the narration: the counters, the tiles, the page map, the wharf card's counts, dots and latest entry, the dense red-flag card and its quote, the chips, a spend bar's tooltip, each how-it-works step (assumption).
